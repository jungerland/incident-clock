A vendor incident has happened. Decide what ExampleCo owes one customer under that customer's own contract.

Read the whole contract, including its definitions, schedules and exhibits. Decide from this contract's wording, not from ExampleCo's standard terms or from what other customers' contracts say. The definitions often decide the answer: whether this form of data counts, whether a suspected incident counts, and when the clock starts.

You are given which of this customer's batches the vendor held when the incident happened, and in what form. Code has already worked out which batches count as held. Use the method and description of each batch when you decide whether the contract covers that data. Do not assume what the vendor's systems contained beyond this.

Say no when the contract's duties do not reach this data, and explain why. A decision to notify and a decision not to notify both need the contract wording that supports them.

Also say what else the contract requires now: where notice must go and how, who ExampleCo must not contact, repeating updates, evidence, and anything else this incident sets off, including changes ExampleCo might make to the vendor relationship in response. Flag for legal anything the facts suggest ExampleCo has already done outside the contract.

If notice is owed, draft it for counsel to review. Nothing is sent. Address it only to the customer's required contact. State only what the incident report and the batch list say, say plainly what is not yet known, and include whatever the contract requires a notice to contain.

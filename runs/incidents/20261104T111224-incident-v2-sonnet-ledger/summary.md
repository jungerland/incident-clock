**Contract wording behind the decision**
- The tokenized analyst feedback batches at Labelforge are Bank Data. (2.2): ""Bank Data" means all data and information, in any form, that Supplier or any Subcontractor receives, accesses, creates or processes in connection with the Agreement, including (a) data provided by or on behalf of Bank or its customers; (b) metadata, logs and configuration data relating to Bank's use of the Services; and (c) any data derived from any of the foregoing, including model outputs, embeddings, annotations, and data that has been aggregated, de-identified, pseudonymized or tokenized."
- Labelforge is a Subcontractor. (2.5): ""Subcontractor" means any third party, including any Supplier Affiliate, that Supplier engages to perform any part of the Services or that has access to Bank Data."
- Labelforge is an Approved Subcontractor for these exact feedback batches. (Schedule 2): "Only de-identified feedback samples that Bank submits through the Services' feedback feature, for improving Bank's own agents. Bank Data in identifiable form is not permitted."
- A login with a stolen credential to Labelforge's annotation platform counts, even though it is not yet known what was viewed. (2.4): ""Security Incident" means any actual or reasonably suspected (a) unauthorized access to, or acquisition, use, disclosure, alteration or destruction of, Bank Data; or (b) unauthorized access to any system of Supplier or any Subcontractor that stores, processes or transmits Bank Data, whether or not unauthorized access to Bank Data has been confirmed."
- Labelforge's own detection starts the clock. (2.3): ""Discovery" means the earliest time at which Supplier or any Subcontractor knew, or by exercising reasonable diligence would have known, of a Security Incident."
- This is the notice duty and time limit. (6.1): "Supplier shall notify Bank of any Security Incident as soon as possible and in no event later than twelve (12) hours after Discovery."
- The 12-hour period controls over the 72-hour period in Exhibit D. (6.8): "Where this Addendum, the Agreement, any exhibit or schedule, or applicable law specifies more than one period for the same notice, the shortest period applies."
- Longer periods in any ExampleCo DPA do not apply. (1.2): "This Addendum controls over any conflicting term of the Agreement, any order form, and any data processing addendum, privacy policy, or other document published or provided by Supplier."

(5.3): "Supplier shall require each Subcontractor to notify Supplier of any Security Incident within six (6) hours after the Subcontractor's Discovery."

**Repeating duties**

**Contract wording behind the decision**
- Logs and traces count, and so does derived data. The definition is not limited to personal data, so hashing the identifiers does not take batch LF-2026-0818 out of scope. (2.2): ""Retailer Data" means all data that Retailer, its employees or its customers provide to Vendor or that Vendor collects from Retailer's systems, including telemetry, logs, traces and metrics, and all data derived from any of it."
- Suspected access is enough. It is not yet known whether the data was viewed, but a login to the platform holding the batch with a stolen credential qualifies. (2.3): ""Data Incident" means any actual or reasonably suspected unauthorized access to, or acquisition, disclosure or loss of, Retailer Data, including while held by a Subprocessor."
- Labelforge annotates Retailer Data to improve detectors for ARG, which is processing on Vendor's behalf, so incidents at Labelforge are covered. (2.4): ""Subprocessor" means any third party, including any Vendor Affiliate, that processes Retailer Data on Vendor's behalf."
- Notice duty and deadline. The clock runs from Vendor's (XYZwire's) awareness. (6.1): "Vendor shall notify Retailer of a Data Incident without undue delay and no later than twenty-four (24) hours after Vendor becomes aware of it."
- ExampleCo's standard terms, such as a longer notice window, do not apply. (1.1): "This VDPA prevails over the Subscription Agreement and over any data processing terms, policies or online terms of Vendor or its Affiliates."
- The ExampleCo acquisition does not change the 24-hour duty. (9.2): "Any acquirer's standard terms do not apply to Retailer Data unless Retailer agrees in writing."

## Amazing Health System

**Contract wording behind the decision**
- Safe Harbor data falls outside the BAA, including its reporting duties. (2.3): "Information de-identified in accordance with this Section 2.3 is not PHI and is not subject to this BAA."
- Both batches used this method: all 18 identifiers removed and reviewed by ExampleCo Privacy. (2.3): "Business Associate may de-identify PHI only in accordance with the safe harbor method at 45 C.F.R. § 164.514(b)(2)"
- A Security Incident report under 4.2 requires ePHI, and none was held. (1.2): ""Security Incident" has the meaning given in 45 C.F.R. § 164.304, limited to incidents involving ePHI that Business Associate or its Subcontractors create, receive, maintain or transmit for Covered Entity."
- The breach duty is tied to Unsecured PHI, which is not present here. (4.1): "Business Associate shall report to Covered Entity any Breach of Unsecured PHI without unreasonable delay and in no event later than ten (10) calendar days after Discovery."
- The impermissible-disclosure duty also requires PHI. (4.3): "Business Associate shall report to Covered Entity any use or disclosure of PHI not provided for by this BAA within ten (10) calendar days after becoming aware of it."
- PHI takes its HIPAA meaning, and validly de-identified data is not PHI under HIPAA. (1.1): "Terms used but not defined in this BAA have the meanings given in the HIPAA Rules (45 C.F.R. Parts 160 and 164), including "Breach," "Discovery," "Protected Health Information" ("PHI"), "Electronic Protected Health Information" ("ePHI") and "Unsecured Protected Health Information.""

**Open questions**
- Is the Safe Harbor de-identification valid for both batches? Free-text reviewer feedback on claims outputs can carry dates, IDs or names, and Safe Harbor also requires no actual knowledge that the remaining data could identify someone.
- Did Labelforge's annotation platform hold any other AHS data, such as raw claims, a limited data set (PHI under 2.4) or a re-identification key, beyond the two listed batches?
- What data was viewed or downloaded? Labelforge has not yet determined this.
- If PHI turns out to be involved, whose awareness starts the 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3 clocks? Under HIPAA, Discovery can be imputed from an agent, so Labelforge's own detection at 06:00 Nov 2 could count rather than ExampleCo being told at 15:00. Legal should also decide whether 4.1 or the shorter 4.2 period governs.
- Would counsel want to send AHS a voluntary courtesy notice for relationship reasons, even though the BAA does not require one?
- Does the de-identified-data use limit in 2.3 survive the statement that such data is 'not subject to this BAA'? This matters if Labelforge's handling goes beyond improving AHS services.

Batches held: LF-2026-0602, LF-2026-0915. Deleted before the incident: none.
